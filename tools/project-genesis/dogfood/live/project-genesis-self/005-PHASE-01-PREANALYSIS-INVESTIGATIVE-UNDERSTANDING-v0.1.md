# Phase 01 — Pre-analysis Investigative Understanding

Status: `DOGFOOD / PROTOCOL-DRAFT / UNVALIDATED`
Date: 2026-09-07
Subject: Project Genesis analyzing Project Genesis

## Purpose

Establish a protected first phase whose sole objective is to understand an early idea as faithfully as possible before commercial, technical, feasibility, novelty, sourcing, implementation or desirability evaluation is allowed.

This phase exists because an early idea can be damaged by the analyst before the analysis even begins: the analyst may narrow, normalize, reinterpret, complete gaps using their own assumptions, compare against the wrong category, or reject an idea that was never actually understood.

The protocol therefore treats **misunderstanding-induced idea loss** as a first-class risk.

## Core law

> **NO EVALUATION BEFORE UNDERSTANDING SUFFICIENCY.**

The investigator may clarify, restate, expose ambiguity, map alternatives of interpretation and request examples. The investigator may not yet decide whether the idea is good, bad, viable, marketable, original, useful, feasible, fundable, scalable or worth pursuing.

The purpose is not to protect the idea from future criticism. The purpose is to protect the future criticism from being applied to the wrong idea.

## Epistemic separation

```text
PHASE 01 — UNDERSTAND
    ↓
UNDERSTANDING SUFFICIENCY GATE
    ↓
PHASE 02+ — INVESTIGATE / COMPARE / FACTOR / EVALUATE EVIDENCE
```

During Phase 01, the investigator must not import downstream conclusions into the interview.

## Primary risk model

The phase is risk-led. The protocol prioritizes preventing these failures:

### R1 — Premature closure

The analyst forms an early interpretation and stops exploring alternative meanings.

### R2 — Confirmation bias

The analyst asks questions that selectively confirm the first interpretation.

### R3 — Leading-question distortion

The analyst inserts concepts, constraints, categories or examples that the originator did not intend.

### R4 — Category capture

The idea is prematurely classified as an existing product/category and subsequent questions are constrained by that category.

### R5 — Feasibility contamination

Questions about cost, technology, market or implementation silently reshape what the idea is before its intended meaning is understood.

### R6 — Commercial contamination

The idea is judged through current market assumptions before the intended future state, problem or transformation is understood.

### R7 — Gap filling by the analyst

Ambiguities are silently completed using analyst assumptions rather than marked as `UNKNOWN` or returned to the originator.

### R8 — Novelty aversion under uncertainty

Unfamiliarity or uncertainty causes the analyst to prefer practical/familiar interpretations and undervalue unfamiliar ones.

### R9 — Loss of genealogy

Changes caused by clarification are not distinguished from the original idea, making it impossible to know whether the analyst understood, corrected or altered the idea.

### R10 — Evaluation leakage

Language such as `viable`, `unrealistic`, `already exists`, `good idea`, `bad idea`, `too broad`, `too expensive`, `nobody would use`, or equivalent enters before the understanding gate.

## Adopted prior art

This draft does not claim that protection against premature evaluation is original.

We explicitly adopt and adapt established findings and practices:

1. **Bias against creativity under uncertainty** — Mueller, Melwani & Goncalo (2012), DOI `10.1177/0956797611421018`, found that uncertainty can activate negative bias against creative ideas and interfere with recognition of creativity.
2. **Early ideas are incomplete and perceived differently** — Sukhov (2018), DOI `10.1111/caim.12262`, reports that incomplete early ideas expose evaluation to cognitive bias and that gap-filling can lead to undervaluation.
3. **Idea evaluation as development, not only gatekeeping** — Sukhov et al. / Design Science (2023) argues that treating evaluation only as a decision gate can create fixation and that early ideas require interpretation, elaboration and transformation.
4. **Problem framing and questioning matter** — Cardoso, Badke-Schaub & Eris (2016), DOI `10.1016/j.destud.2016.07.002`, found that high-level questions can facilitate reframing and counter fixation.
5. **Open questioning can reduce confirmation-bias effects** — Powell & Hughes-Scholes (2012), DOI `10.1002/jip.1357`, in investigative interviewing, found that skilled adherence to open questions reduced the effect of prior-biased information on leading-question use. Transfer to project-idea interviewing is a hypothesis, not an established direct result.
6. **Defer judgment during divergent work** — brainstorming/design-thinking traditions separate generation/exploration from convergence/evaluation. We adopt this as a sequencing principle, not as proof of our exact protocol.
7. **Idea journey includes elaboration before implementation** — Perry-Smith & Mannucci (2017), DOI `10.5465/amr.2014.0462`, distinguish idea generation, elaboration, championing and implementation.

A 2026 working paper uses the terms `premature closure` and `interpretive suspension` for early-stage idea survival. It is relevant adjacent work but is not treated here as validated authority until peer-review/status are verified.

## Interview behavior

The conductor must behave as an investigator of meaning, not as an evaluator.

### Allowed

- open invitations: `explique do seu jeito`;
- requests for concrete examples;
- contrast questions: `o que isso é / o que isso não é?`;
- temporal questions: `o que acontece antes / depois?`;
- actor questions: `quem participa e o que muda para cada ator?`;
- counterexample questions;
- reflective restatement explicitly marked as provisional;
- multiple competing interpretations;
- asking the originator to correct the model;
- marking unknowns instead of filling them;
- asking whether an apparent contradiction is real or only linguistic;
- preserving exact terms supplied by the originator.

### Forbidden before the gate

- market sizing;
- competitor comparison;
- technical feasibility judgment;
- architecture selection;
- cost estimation;
- business-model recommendation;
- novelty/exclusivity claims;
- GO/NO-GO;
- sourcing classification;
- PMFN factorization intended to decide build/adopt;
- naming/branding;
- solution optimization;
- replacing the originator's concept with a more conventional one.

## Interview progression

The phase is conversational, not a rigid questionnaire. The following sequence is a coverage map.

### I1 — Uninterrupted exposition

First ask the originator to explain the idea in their own order.

Do not interrupt to correct, evaluate or optimize. Record unresolved expressions for later.

### I2 — Reflective reconstruction

Produce a provisional reconstruction beginning with:

> `Minha compreensão provisória é...`

Separate:
- what the originator explicitly said;
- what the conductor inferred;
- what remains unknown.

The originator must be able to reject the reconstruction without friction.

### I3 — Boundary discovery

Explore:
- intended transformation;
- actors;
- inputs/outputs;
- before/after state;
- explicit exclusions;
- examples and counterexamples;
- invariants the originator refuses to lose.

### I4 — Ambiguity expansion

For every high-impact ambiguity, generate at least two plausible interpretations when possible and ask which, if either, is intended.

The conductor must not silently choose one.

### I5 — Consequence probes without judgment

Ask what would follow **if the intended idea worked as conceived**, without asking whether it can work.

This protects radical propositions from being prematurely reduced to current feasibility assumptions.

### I6 — Contradiction reconciliation

Where two statements appear inconsistent, present both and ask whether:
- one supersedes the other;
- they apply to different contexts;
- the apparent conflict results from terminology;
- the idea intentionally contains the tension.

### I7 — Originator validation

Return the structured model to the originator and ask explicitly:

1. `O que eu entendi errado?`
2. `O que está faltando e seria perigoso omitir?`
3. `O que eu acrescentei que não veio de você?`
4. `Qual interpretação minha poderia matar ou desviar a ideia se eu a levasse para a análise?`
5. `Existe algo que parece absurdo sob premissas atuais, mas faz parte intencional da ideia e deve ser preservado para investigação posterior?`

## Understanding Sufficiency Gate

Phase 01 does not end because the conductor feels confident.

It ends only when the originator explicitly homologates a representation containing at least:

```text
ORIGINAL EXPOSITION
CURRENT IDEA STATEMENT
INTENDED TRANSFORMATION
ACTORS
BOUNDARIES
NON-GOALS
INVARIANTS
EXAMPLES
COUNTEREXAMPLES
KNOWN FACTS FROM ORIGINATOR
INFERENCES BY CONDUCTOR
UNKNOWNS
AMBIGUITIES RESOLVED
AMBIGUITIES STILL OPEN
IDEA CHANGES DURING INTERVIEW
MISINTERPRETATION RISKS
ORIGINATOR HOMOLOGATION
```

Possible gate states:

```text
NOT_READY
READY_WITH_OPEN_UNKNOWNS
READY
```

`READY_WITH_OPEN_UNKNOWNS` is valid when the unknowns do not prevent faithful representation and are explicitly preserved for later investigation.

## Phase output

The mandatory output is a **Pre-analysis Investigative Understanding Record**.

It is not a viability report.

It must be suitable as the immutable input to the next investigative phase.

## Dogfood implication

Project Genesis itself must now pass through this phase before deeper viability research continues.

The current live dogfood documents are evidence and hypotheses, but they do not substitute for a formally homologated Phase 01 understanding record.

## Implementation requirements discovered

- `IR-010` — runtime must support an evaluation embargo state.
- `IR-011` — every statement must be classifiable as originator fact, conductor inference, unknown or later external evidence.
- `IR-012` — conductor must preserve competing interpretations until originator resolution.
- `IR-013` — originator homologation is required before downstream analysis.
- `IR-014` — genealogy must distinguish clarification from alteration.
- `IR-015` — downstream agents must receive the homologated record, not reconstruct the idea independently from raw chat unless explicitly tasked.
- `IR-016` — protocol must detect and flag evaluation leakage during Phase 01.
- `IR-017` — interview output must include explicit misinterpretation-risk analysis.

## Research opportunity

The broad phenomenon is already supported by prior literature: uncertainty-related bias against creativity, incomplete-idea comprehension effects, fixation, framing, and the value of elaboration before selection all exist.

The research question that may remain is narrower:

> Can a reproducible, AI-mediated pre-analysis interview protocol reduce distortion of early project ideas before evidence-based viability/sourcing analysis, and can the reduction be measured through originator-validated semantic fidelity, lower leading-question contamination, fewer unsupported analyst insertions and downstream decision stability?

Novelty is **not authorized**. A systematic mapping/review is required before claiming an original scientific contribution.
