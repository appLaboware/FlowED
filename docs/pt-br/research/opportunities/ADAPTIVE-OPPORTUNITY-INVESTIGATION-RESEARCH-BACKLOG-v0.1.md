# Adaptive Opportunity Investigation — Research Backlog v0.1

Status: `RESEARCH OPPORTUNITY REGISTER / NOVELTY NOT ESTABLISHED`

## Adopted baseline

Project Genesis must treat entrepreneurial opportunity recognition and entrepreneurial alertness as established antecedent regions.

Key starting point: Tang, Kacmar & Busenitz (2012), DOI `10.1016/j.jbusvent.2010.07.001`, models entrepreneurial alertness through scanning/search, association/connection and evaluation/judgment.

George et al. (2016), DOI `10.1007/s11365-014-0347-y`, systematically reviews 180 opportunity-recognition articles and identifies prior knowledge, social capital, cognition/personality, environmental conditions, alertness and systematic search as influential factors.

Sharma (2019), DOI `10.1108/JEEE-05-2018-0049`, systematically reviews entrepreneurial alertness and identifies sensing/searching information, cognition, personality, environment, networks, knowledge and experience among core components.

Lanivich et al. (2022), DOI `10.1016/j.jbusres.2021.10.023`, reports conceptual and operational ambiguity in entrepreneurial alertness and identifies future research directions.

Araujo et al. (2023), DOI `10.1016/j.jbvi.2023.e00394`, meta-analyzes 125 empirical studies and reports positive relationships between entrepreneurial alertness and opportunity recognition, innovation and other entrepreneurial outcomes.

These references establish a research north. They do not validate the Project Genesis mechanism proposed below.

## Residual hypothesis under investigation

A discovery protocol may improve relevance by allocating additional research depth to regions of explicit or inferred originator interest while maintaining a mandatory broad lateral scan capable of discovering opportunities the originator did not identify.

The residual is the governed combination, not any isolated component.

## D1 — Doctoral opportunity: Adaptive Breadth–Depth Opportunity Investigation

Candidate research question:

> Can an evidence-governed investigative protocol dynamically allocate research depth from explicit and discourse-derived originator interest while preserving minimum broad opportunity coverage, and does this improve relevant opportunity discovery without increasing omission, anchoring or investigator projection?

Potential contribution dimensions:

- formal breadth/depth allocation policy;
- minimum lateral-scan guarantee;
- provenance of research-depth decisions;
- separation of explicit interest, inferred interest and externally discovered opportunity;
- anti-anchoring controls;
- measurable trade-off between relevance and discovery breadth.

Novelty status: `NOT_ESTABLISHED`.

Required next work:

- systematic mapping/review across entrepreneurship, information seeking, conversational elicitation, recommender systems, requirements elicitation and adaptive search;
- construct definition;
- measurement model;
- controlled and field experiments;
- cross-domain replication.

## D2 — Doctoral opportunity: Reference-Context-Governed Multi-Agent Investigation

Candidate research question:

> Does a versioned evidence-based reference context improve multi-agent research efficiency and compliance without suppressing independent discovery, and which disclosure strategy best controls anchoring?

Compare at least:

```text
A — no shared reference context
B — RCB informed before search
C — blinded-first search followed by RCB reconciliation
```

Potential dependent variables:

- verified useful claims;
- duplicated rediscovery;
- unsupported claims;
- source quality;
- counterevidence found;
- unique external discoveries;
- convergence caused by evidence versus shared anchoring;
- time/inference cost;
- baseline corrections discovered.

Novelty status: `NOT_ESTABLISHED`.

## Article opportunities

### A1 — Broad Scan vs Interest-Weighted Deep Search

Experimentally compare uniform research allocation against interest-weighted depth with a protected lateral scan.

### A2 — Explicit vs Inferred Originator Interest

Measure whether discourse-derived interest profiles agree with originator self-report and whether disagreement causes research distortion.

### A3 — Reference Context and Anchoring in LLM Research Agents

Compare RCB-informed and blinded-first agents for omission, convergence, contradiction discovery and source diversity.

### A4 — Provenance-Aware Opportunity Discovery

Evaluate whether labeling opportunities as `ORIGINATOR_EXPLICIT`, `INTEREST_INFERRED`, `BOUNDARY_DERIVED`, `AGENT_PROPOSED` improves human review and prevents retrospective rewriting of idea genealogy.

### A5 — Cross-Domain Generalization

Use the three planned dogfood cases: reflexive software/method research, educational digital product, and planned-furniture service. Determine which rules are case-specific, domain-specific, cross-domain or core candidates.

## Initial tests

### T1 — Interest-profile fidelity

Blindly derive an interest profile from Phase 01, then ask the originator to rate agreement. Measure precision/recall or ranked agreement after an appropriate coding scheme is validated.

### T2 — Lateral-opportunity retention

Compare whether an interest-weighted process still discovers valuable opportunities outside emphasized regions.

### T3 — Search-depth gain

Hold total research budget approximately constant and compare uniform versus adaptive allocation.

### T4 — Anchoring by context base

Compare RCB-informed versus blinded-first investigators.

### T5 — Counterevidence resilience

Seed a baseline containing a deliberately disputable provisional claim and measure whether investigators challenge it.

### T6 — Genealogy preservation

Test whether reviewers can correctly distinguish original intent from later discovered opportunities using provenance labels.
