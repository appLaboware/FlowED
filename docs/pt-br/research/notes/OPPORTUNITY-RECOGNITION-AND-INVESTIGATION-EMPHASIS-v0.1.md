# Opportunity Recognition and Investigation Emphasis v0.1

Status: `RESEARCH NOTE / SUPPORTING PRIOR ART / NOT A NOVELTY CLAIM`

## Why this note exists

Project Genesis needs to distinguish between:

- what the originator explicitly asks to investigate;
- what the originator appears to care about most from repeated emphasis;
- opportunities not mentioned by the originator but potentially relevant;
- directions explicitly excluded by the originator.

The protocol must not make unmentioned opportunities disappear. It may, however, allocate deeper search effort to regions that matter more to the originator.

## Relevant prior-art regions

### Entrepreneurial alertness

Tang, Kacmar & Busenitz (2012), Journal of Business Venturing, DOI `10.1016/j.jbusvent.2010.07.001`, operationalize entrepreneurial alertness through three dimensions: scanning/search, association/connection, and evaluation/judgment. This supports treating opportunity recognition as an active process rather than waiting for the originator to name every opportunity.

Valliere (2013), Journal of Business Venturing, DOI `10.1016/j.jbusvent.2011.08.004`, models entrepreneurial alertness through schema-mediated interpretation of environmental change. This is relevant to the idea that different attention structures can surface different opportunities.

A 2023 meta-analysis in Journal of Business Venturing Insights, DOI `10.1016/j.jbvi.2023.e00394`, synthesizes 125 studies and reports positive association of entrepreneurial alertness with opportunity recognition, innovation and performance. This supports using explicit opportunity-search mechanisms, while not proving Project Genesis's specific protocol.

Lanivich et al. (2022), Journal of Business Research, DOI `10.1016/j.jbusres.2021.10.023`, review and synthesize entrepreneurial alertness and note continuing conceptual/measurement ambiguity. Project Genesis should therefore adopt the broad insight without pretending that a single settled operationalization exists.

### Opportunity identification through means-oriented search

A randomized controlled field experiment published in Academy of Management Learning & Education, DOI `10.5465/amle.2017.0092`, found that both creativity training and effectuation-oriented training improved opportunity identification, with the effectuation training showing larger increases in the number of opportunities identified and pursued in that sample. This supports the broader idea that opportunity search can be actively structured.

## Protocol implications

1. `UNMENTIONED != IRRELEVANT`.
2. Explicit originator interest may legitimately increase search depth.
3. Repeated emphasis may create a provisional `IMPLICIT_INTEREST_PROFILE`, but it must remain an inference until confirmed.
4. Search emphasis must not become search exclusion.
5. Opportunity discovery should remain open beyond the originator's initial vocabulary unless a direction is explicitly forbidden.
6. A later opportunity must retain provenance: `ORIGINATOR_EXPLICIT`, `CONDUCTOR_INFERRED_INTEREST`, `BOUNDARY_DERIVED`, `CONSULTANT_PROPOSED`, or another declared source class.
7. The protocol should later test whether emphasis-weighted search improves useful discovery without causing tunnel vision.

## Research caution

The cited literature concerns entrepreneurship, alertness, opportunity recognition and opportunity-identification training. It does not directly validate the exact Project Genesis mechanism of inferring an interest profile from conversational emphasis and using it to weight a multi-source boundary investigation. That remains a protocol hypothesis to test in dogfood and external cases.
